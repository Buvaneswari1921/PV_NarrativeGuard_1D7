PV_NarrativeGuard - Safety Narrative Quality Guidance
DEMO / SYNTHETIC KNOWLEDGE BASE

Purpose
-------
This document provides demonstration guidance for reviewing
the quality of AI-generated pharmacovigilance safety narratives.

Quality Review Principles
--------------------------
A generated safety narrative should be reviewed for accuracy,
completeness, consistency, clarity, and appropriate use of
source information.

The narrative should remain faithful to the underlying ICSR.

Accuracy
--------
The narrative should accurately represent information contained
in the source case.

Reviewers should verify:

- Patient information
- Suspect medicinal product
- Adverse event
- Event date
- Seriousness
- Outcome
- Clinical information
- Reporter information

Unsupported information should not be introduced.

Completeness
------------
The narrative should contain the important information available
in the source ICSR.

Missing important clinical information should be identified
during quality review.

Chronology and Consistency
--------------------------
Dates and clinical events should follow a logical sequence.

The narrative should not contain contradictions between different
parts of the source case.

For example, the narrative should not describe a patient as
recovered if the source case indicates that the outcome is
recovering.

Grounding
---------
Every factual statement in the generated narrative should be
supported by the source ICSR or by clearly identified supporting
information.

The system should flag statements that cannot be supported by
the available evidence.

Clarity
-------
The narrative should be concise, readable, and medically
meaningful.

Unnecessary repetition should be avoided.

Human Quality Review
--------------------
AI-generated narratives should be reviewed by an appropriate
pharmacovigilance professional before final use.

The reviewer should verify the generated narrative against the
source ICSR and supporting evidence.

QC Outcome
----------
The quality review may identify:

- Pass
- Pass with minor revision
- Requires major revision
- Unsupported information
- Missing important information
- Contradictory information

The QC process is intended to assist human review rather than
replace pharmacovigilance judgment.
