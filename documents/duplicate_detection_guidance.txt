PV_NarrativeGuard - Duplicate Detection Guidance
DEMO / SYNTHETIC KNOWLEDGE BASE

Purpose
-------
This document provides demonstration guidance for assessing
potential duplicate Individual Case Safety Reports (ICSRs).

Duplicate Assessment Principles
-------------------------------
A potential duplicate should be assessed using multiple pieces
of evidence rather than relying on a single matching field.

Relevant case attributes may include:

- Patient age
- Patient sex
- Country
- Suspect medicinal product
- Indication
- Adverse event
- Event date
- Seriousness
- Outcome
- Reporter type
- Clinical description

Structured Evidence
-------------------
Exact matches in structured fields can provide useful evidence
when screening an incoming ICSR against historical cases.

Important fields such as patient demographics, suspect drug,
adverse event, and event date should be considered together.

Semantic Evidence
-----------------
Two reports may describe the same clinical event using different
terminology.

Examples:

"Acute kidney injury" and "acute renal impairment" may describe
similar clinical concepts.

"Hypoglycemia" and "low blood glucose" may describe similar
clinical concepts.

Semantic similarity can therefore be used to identify candidate
cases that may not be detected through exact text matching.

Potential False Positives
--------------------------
High similarity does not automatically mean that two reports
are duplicates.

Two reports may contain the same medicinal product and similar
adverse events while representing different patients or
different clinical events.

Differences in patient demographics, event dates, clinical
details, or other case information should be considered during
the investigation.

Human Review
------------
Potential duplicates should be reviewed by a qualified
pharmacovigilance professional before a final duplicate
decision is made.

The system should support the review by presenting relevant
evidence and candidate cases.

The system should not automatically merge, delete, or close
cases solely because a similarity score exceeds a threshold.

Decision Support
----------------
Duplicate detection should be treated as an investigation
support process.

The AI system should provide:

1. Candidate historical cases
2. Structured matching evidence
3. Semantic similarity evidence
4. Relevant supporting guidance
5. A clear explanation of why a case requires review

The final duplicate determination remains with the
appropriate human pharmacovigilance reviewer.
