PV_NarrativeGuard - Safety Narrative Generation Guidance
DEMO / SYNTHETIC KNOWLEDGE BASE

Purpose
-------
This document provides demonstration guidance for generating
structured safety narratives from Individual Case Safety Reports
(ICSRs).

Narrative Principles
--------------------
A safety narrative should present the relevant clinical information
in a clear, concise, chronological, and medically meaningful manner.

The narrative should be based only on information available in
the source ICSR and relevant supporting evidence.

Core Information
----------------
A safety narrative may include:

- Patient demographics
- Relevant medical history or indication
- Suspect medicinal product
- Relevant treatment information
- Adverse event
- Event onset or event date
- Seriousness
- Clinical course
- Outcome
- Relevant investigations
- Treatment provided for the event
- Reporter information
- Relevant follow-up information

Chronological Structure
-----------------------
Where dates are available, information should be presented in a
logical chronological sequence.

The narrative should distinguish between:

1. Information known before the event
2. Event onset and clinical presentation
3. Investigations and treatment
4. Clinical course
5. Outcome and follow-up

Source Grounding
----------------
The narrative must remain grounded in the source case.

The system should not invent:

- Symptoms
- Laboratory results
- Diagnoses
- Treatments
- Dates
- Patient history
- Clinical outcomes
- Causality conclusions

If important information is unavailable, the narrative should
not fabricate the missing information.

AI-Assisted Generation
----------------------
An AI model may assist with organizing and summarizing the case,
but the generated narrative should be treated as draft content.

The AI should use relevant case information and supporting
knowledge retrieved by the RAG system.

Human Review
------------
A qualified pharmacovigilance professional should review the
generated narrative before it is finalized.

The reviewer should verify that:

- The narrative accurately represents the source ICSR.
- Important clinical information has not been omitted.
- No unsupported information has been introduced.
- Dates and chronology are consistent.
- The wording is clear and appropriate.
- The narrative does not change the meaning of the source case.

Narrative Output
----------------
The system should produce a concise narrative that allows a
reviewer to understand the relevant clinical course without
having to reconstruct the case manually from multiple fields.

The generated narrative is a decision-support artifact and
should not replace human pharmacovigilance review.
